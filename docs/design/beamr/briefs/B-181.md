# B-181: Tickless idle: an idle worker parks with no timeout unless a timer is armed, and every event source wakes it

> **Cluster:** beamr
> **Checklist:**
> - C222 — §3.8 carries a reviewed wake-edge enumeration of every event that can make an idle worker's work runnable, each with its file, line, today's notify and its edge, written before the code (B-181 R1).
> - C223 — A wake sequence advanced under the wait_set lock by every wake source and compared by park_thread under that lock means no wake between a worker's checks and its park is lost (B-181 R2).
> - C224 — An idle worker parks with no timeout, or until the earliest armed timer deadline, and file-IO completions and every other polled source wake it; IDLE_PARK_TIMEOUT is gone (B-181 R3).
> - C225 — §3.8's amendment retires the 5 ms floor for a bound of zero idle wakes, in its own words with its reason, left for Waffles's re-sign, and the CHANGELOG says so (B-181 R4).
> **Stories:**
> - S44 (Embedder, Running beamr under a long-lived host process) — As an embedder whose process is idle most of the time, I want an idle beamr scheduler to sleep until there is work or an armed timer is due, so that an idle process costs no CPU and no wake-ups, and no wake is ever lost.

## Purpose

The named follow-on commit the Q-F ruling of 2026-07-11 left open (docs/EMBEDDER-COMPOSITION-SPEC.md §3.8, and its sequence item 8), now asked for by the Tharsis core builder through Scott and Waffles on 21 October 2026, and written by Apollo on Waffles's word with Hermes's four lines as its shape. At beamr 0.21.0 (aeabb126) an idle normal worker parks in park_thread on wake_condvar.wait_timeout(IDLE_PARK_TIMEOUT), 5 ms (crates/beamr/src/scheduler/execution.rs:974-1017), because two event sources are polled by loop iterations rather than notifying the condvar: the timer wheel, ticked by worker 0 (timer_integration::tick_timers, timer_integration.rs:93-96), and the file-IO ring's completions, drained by worker 0 (drain_file_io_completions, execution.rs:575-600, poll_completions with a zero timeout). So every idle worker of every scheduler wakes up to 200 times a second forever. The Tharsis builder measured one idle Tharsis store on Tom's Mac (two haematite schedulers, 30 normal workers) at 4.4 to 4.6% CPU. Since the ruling, Tom's rule that no wait is of a set length (no fixed waits, ever) has been given, and a timed park whose only purpose is to poll is such a wait; the signed 5 ms bound predates the rule. A second hazard follows from removing the timeout and is the centre of this brief: a worker checks its run queue, its injected spawns and the steal targets before it takes the wait_set lock in park_thread (execution.rs:420-470 against :994-1000), so a wake that lands in that gap is lost, and today the 5 ms timeout hides the loss; with no timeout it would hang the process. Every wake source must therefore be ordered against the park, not only given a notify.

## Task

Write the wake-edge enumeration the Q-F ruling requires first, as a section of docs/EMBEDDER-COMPOSITION-SPEC.md §3.8; order every wake against the park by a wake sequence read before a worker's checks and compared under the wait_set lock; make park_thread wait with no timeout when no timer is armed and no IO is in flight, and only until the earliest timer deadline when one is armed; give the file-IO ring's completions a wake edge; amend §3.8's signed bound in its own words for Waffles's re-sign; and say it in the CHANGELOG. The work is in crates/beamr/src/scheduler/execution.rs, timer_integration.rs, mod.rs, crates/beamr/src/timer.rs and crates/beamr/src/io/thread_pool.rs, with tests in a new crates/beamr/src/scheduler/tickless_idle_tests.rs.

## Requirements

### R1: Every event that can make an idle worker's work runnable is enumerated with its wake edge, before any code changes

Documentary, first. docs/EMBEDDER-COMPOSITION-SPEC.md §3.8 SHALL gain a wake-edge enumeration section listing every event that can make work runnable on an idle normal worker: at least a process woken by a message or a link or monitor signal (wake_process, execution.rs:525-535, and its callers), a spawn injected into a worker's queue (spawning.rs:492, :504, :525), a fired timer (timer_integration.rs), a file-IO completion (drain_file_io_completions), a standard-IO or generic-IO completion, a dirty-call or host-await completion resuming a suspended process, the readiness service's deliveries, the distribution manager's deliveries, a supervision signal (supervision_integration.rs) and shutdown (execution.rs:126-131, timer_integration.rs:78-89), a timer armed with a deadline earlier than the one a parked worker 0 waits for (timer.rs:142-175, reached from native contexts holding only the wheel and from the public Scheduler::timers()), and work stealing between workers; for each, the file and line where the event happens, whether it notifies wake_condvar today and under which lock, and the edge it gets under this brief. Any source found that cannot be given a clean wake edge SHALL be named there, and the Q-F ruling's recorded fallback (adaptive backoff) SHALL then be decided in the open for that source only, never silently. The enumeration SHALL be reviewed (Hermes) before R2's code is written. Every wake under this brief SHALL stay notify_all on wake_condvar, or, where a source is given a narrower wake, SHALL wake worker 0 by name: worker 0 alone ticks timers and drains file-IO completions, so a notify_one, or any later targeted wake, that reached another worker could leave a deadline or a completion stranded under an unbounded park. The enumeration SHALL carry this as an invariant with that reason (Hermes's review, 21 Oct). The enumeration's first draft (§3.8.1, rows W1 to W24) found work stealing (W23) without a clean edge; the review (Hermes, Cambium 21 October 2026 04:50, post 1440ba67) decided the surplus edge, and approved the enumeration on the four conditions written in §3.8.2 (C1 the idle count raised before the sequence read, C2 a published parked_until for the arming edge, C3 the lock order timers then wait_set, C4 tick_at's one-lap bound kept exact), which bind R2 and R3.

### R2: No wake is lost between a worker's checks and its park

Behavioural. THE SYSTEM SHALL keep a wake sequence in SharedState that every wake source in R1's enumeration advances under the wait_set lock before it notifies wake_condvar, and park_thread SHALL read that sequence before the worker's run-queue, injection, woken-list and steal checks and SHALL wait only while, under the wait_set lock, the sequence is unchanged; a wake that lands between the checks and the park therefore returns the worker to its loop at once. This SHALL hold for every worker and for worker 0's timer and IO duties, and SHALL hold before R3 removes the timeout, so that R2 is proven with the 5 ms timeout still in place. Work stealing gets the surplus edge of §3.8.2 C1: every worker raises an idle count (SeqCst) before it reads the wake sequence and lowers it on leaving the park; a push that leaves surplus on a queue issues a SeqCst fence, reads the idle count, and runs the edge when it is non-zero; a Requeue after a slice with nothing else queued is not surplus. No path SHALL take the timers lock while holding wait_set (§3.8.2 C3), shown by a search the handback quotes.

**Acceptance:**
- Red first (nextest, beamr, src/scheduler/tickless_idle_tests.rs): with park_thread's timeout raised to an unbounded wait through a test seam, a stress of 10,000 wake_process calls from another thread racing workers that have just found their queues empty runs every woken process, ending on each process's reply; today a wake in the gap is lost and the run hangs (nextest's slow-timeout reports it). ### R3: An idle worker parks with no timeout, or until the earliest armed timer, and the polled sources wake it

Behavioural. THE SYSTEM SHALL park an idle normal worker with no timeout when no timer is armed and no file-IO operation is in flight, and, when a timer is armed, only until the earliest armed deadline, read from the timer wheel by its existing TimerWheel::earliest_deadline (crates/beamr/src/timer.rs:235-238), never a second copy of it; TimerWheel::tick_at (timer.rs:207-220), which steps every millisecond since the last tick under the timers lock, SHALL stop its catch-up after one lap of the wheel's buckets, so a tick after a long unbounded park costs at most one lap; arming a timer earlier than the deadline a parked worker 0 waits for SHALL wake it, through R2's sequence. A file-IO completion SHALL advance R2's sequence and notify wake_condvar as it is published, in every ring type (crates/beamr/src/io/thread_pool.rs:250-251, io/uring.rs:470 and :539, io/ring.rs:220), through a notifier the ring holds, so worker 0 drains it without polling; every other source R1 lists as polled SHALL get its edge here. IDLE_PARK_TIMEOUT and IDLE_WAKES_PER_SEC_PER_WORKER SHALL be removed with the signed bound they served, replaced by R4's. The replay scheduler's park (shared.replay_mode) SHALL keep whatever its own determinism needs, stated in the enumeration. Every wake stays notify_all or wakes worker 0 by name, as R1's invariant says, for the reason it gives. Arming an earlier timer SHALL compare its deadline with a parked_until: Option<Instant> that worker 0 publishes into the wheel under the timers lock when it reads the deadline it parks until, and clears when it leaves the park; schedule_reserved_at runs the edge only when parked_until is set and the new deadline is earlier, an O(1) check, never a scan of the wheel (§3.8.2 C2).

**Acceptance:**
- Red (the same file): on an idle scheduler with no timer armed and no IO in flight, the park timeout observed at the existing seam (observed_park_timeout_millis) is none, an unbounded wait; today it is 5 ms.
- Red: with one timer armed at a deadline D from a process on a worker other than worker 0, the observed park timeout of worker 0 is the time to D, and the receive-after it serves fires, ending on the process's timeout reply; a test seam counting the parks that ended by timing out (wait_timeout's timed_out()) shows, between the arming and the timeout reply, exactly 1 for worker 0 and 0 for every other worker. Today every worker times out every 5 ms until D, so the count is red.
- Red: a process parked on a file read is resumed by the read's completion with every worker parked unbounded, ending on the process's reply.
- Red: a timer armed more than one lap of the wheel out, with worker 0 parked for longer than a lap before its deadline, fires once, at its deadline, never early on the bounded catch-up, and current_tick equals target_tick after the walk (§3.8.2 C4).
- Red: with worker 0 parked until a deadline D, arming a timer at a deadline before D wakes it once and the earlier timer fires at its deadline; arming one after D runs no edge, counted by a seam on the edge.
- A measurement, reported and not asserted, on Tom's Mac with df and swap in START and END: wakes per second of an idle scheduler at default sizing, counted from idle_parks over 10 s, before and after, with every wake after the change counted under its cause (a fired deadline, a cancelled one, a replay cursor advance), so that idle is zero because every wake is accounted for; wakes per message, the parks ended across all workers for one message sent to one parked process, before and after, so the herd notify_all leaves (about 30 workers woken per send at Tharsis's sizing, all but one parking again) is a number for the next brief, named under FOUND; and the idle CPU of one haematite Database before and after.

### R4: §3.8's signed bound is amended in its own words for re-signing, and the CHANGELOG says it

Documentary. docs/EMBEDDER-COMPOSITION-SPEC.md §3.8 SHALL carry an amendment below the Q-F ruling, leaving the ruling's text as it stands, that says: the 5 ms floor is retired because a timed park whose only purpose is to poll is a wait of a set length, which Tom's rule forbids, and the floor was signed before that rule; the bound now signed is zero idle wakes for a scheduler with no armed timer and no IO in flight; with armed timers, one wake per deadline worker 0 reaches, fired or cancelled; in replay, one per cursor advance; over the total workers service_inventory() reports; enforced by R3's tests. Waffles re-signed it as worded here on Cambium on 21 October 2026 at 04:49 (Melbourne), post c38bfbebea87c2c877f87f1eec949b4785a0ba1279ef40fccc020e93f5297ca2, replacing the 04:31 signature (post 8feade12); the amendment SHALL carry the re-sign line with Waffles's name, that date and that post id. CHANGELOG.md SHALL say that idle workers no longer wake and that the 5 ms constants are gone.

## Boundaries

- SHALL NOT change what a process observes: message order, timer semantics, receive-after, links, monitors and exits are unchanged.
- SHALL NOT add a timer, a background poller, or any wait of a set length, in the code or in the tests; a test's wait ends on an event.
- SHALL NOT change the readiness poll thread, which is tickless already, nor the distribution manager beyond giving its deliveries a wake edge if R1 finds one missing.
- SHALL NOT change the wasm cooperative scheduler.
- SHALL NOT add a dependency.
