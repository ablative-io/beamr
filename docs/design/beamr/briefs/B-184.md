## Purpose

Written by hand by Apollo on 4 November 2026, beamr's lead, on Waffles' word that night: "B-184 as you have it, both reds; (b) is the one that matters most, a dead worker must stop the scheduler loudly or be recovered, never look like normal running." Found by Hermes in his first read of liminal-open, the liminal server on Dean's laptop (macOS 26.6.2, 25G83). Everything below was read, nothing run, at beamr main e90ccb0e, in the beamr 0.21.0 and 0.21.1 sources in the cargo registry (diff -rq of their src finds no difference), and in two liminal-open logs. In exchange-20261030T062013Z-f0936ef7.log (liminal f0936ef7, Rust 1.98.1, beamr 0.21.0), lines 51-68 hold six panics, on threads beamr-sched-12, 2, 6, 13, 14 and 9, each "assertion failed: r == libc::ETIMEDOUT || r == 0" at std's sys/pal/unix/sync/condvar.rs:130. That line is the assert after pthread_cond_timedwait_relative_np in std's macOS timed wait (read in the 1.98.1 rust-src, whose rustc commit 48a229ce is the one the panic lines name). The only timed wait a beamr 0.21.x worker makes is park_thread's (crates/beamr/src/scheduler/execution.rs:1012 in 0.21.1): wake_condvar.wait_timeout for IDLE_PARK_TIMEOUT, 5 ms, under the wait_set mutex. Hermes's sample of the wedged process (liminal-open-30084-stuck.sample.txt) shows the nine surviving workers of the fifteen (beamr-sched-0 to 14, thread ids 2495023 to 2495037) all in that call, through _pthread_cond_wait into __psynch_cvwait, the kernel psynch path. So pthread returned an error other than ETIMEDOUT for a valid 5 ms timeout, and std turned it into a panic that ended the worker. The panics carry no timestamp, because liminal-server installs no panic hook (Hermes, 4 November; his brief SRV-061 adds one). The process went on with nine workers. On SIGTERM on 3 November at 12:58:52Z it wedged: the main thread sat in Scheduler::shutdown joining the workers, and a haematite Append on shard 2 waited 70 s and counting, until Hermes killed it. The earlier run was worse. In exchange-20261025T222628Z-f0936ef7.log, lines 48-80 hold eleven of the same panics between 25 October 22:27:00Z and 27 October 10:59:38Z. From 10:59:38Z, Appends on haematite shards 2, 7 and 4 waited and never returned, and the last wait logged was 21,575 s. Line 13010 holds a twelfth panic, beamr-sched-2, at 16:59:03-08Z. For six hours the server stayed up, logged only WARN lines, and took no write. beamr 0.21.x catches nothing at a worker's top (crates/beamr/src/scheduler/mod.rs:1828-1836 spawns scheduler_loop bare): a worker that panics ends its thread, and with it its local run queue and its claim on its inject queue, while every other part of the scheduler goes on as though it had all its workers. Which of those strands the Append is not settled, and R2's red settles it. beamr main also carries B-181 (tickless idle, written and not yet run), which parks workers other than worker 0 on an untimed wait. That path goes through the same psynch call, where std asserts r == 0, so B-181 does not remove this class. Two defects, one brief. The first is that the park can fail at all. The second, which matters most, is that a dead worker looks like normal running.

## Task

Reproduce the park's error return outside liminal and name its cause (R1). Make any worker's death a permanent, loud scheduler failure that every waiter and the embedder see, and that shutdown completes through (R2, R3). Then remove the panic at its cause, by the remedy R1's red names, written as an amendment of this brief before its code (R4). Files: crates/beamr/src/scheduler/mod.rs, execution.rs, execution/park.rs, a new crates/beamr/src/scheduler/failure.rs with its tests, a probe example under crates/beamr/examples, and CHANGELOG.md.

### R2: A worker that dies makes a permanent scheduler failure, raised the moment it dies

Behavioural, red first. The red, written and run before the code: a test-support seam makes one named worker panic at its next park. The test then observes three things at the base. Shutdown on another thread does not complete (the wedge in Hermes's sample, ended by the nextest profile's per-test terminate, which the handback names). A process spawned after the panic either runs or does not, and the handback says which, settling what stranded the Append. Nothing the embedder can read says a worker died. With the code: THE SYSTEM SHALL run every worker under a guard that observes its thread ending by a panic (a drop guard or catch_unwind at the top of the worker's thread, whichever beamr's lint table admits). When it does, the system SHALL latch a permanent failure holding the worker's name and the panic's message, set shutdown, and wake every parked worker and every waiter. The latch is never cleared and has no setting, as beamr-wasm's terminal latch is (crates/beamr-wasm/src/failure.rs, WPORT-7). The embedder SHALL be able to read the failure (Scheduler::failure, returning the latched value or none) and to register one callback that runs once when it latches. A worker that ends by any path other than shutdown SHALL latch the failure too. Lead amendment B (Artemis, beamr lead, 4 November 2026, from Hermes's SRV-061 needs): the callback SHALL be registered by Scheduler::on_failure, taking an FnOnce(&SchedulerFailure) that is Send and 'static. It SHALL run exactly once, on the dying worker's own thread inside its guard, before the other workers and waiters are woken; registered after the latch, it SHALL run at once on the registering thread. The seam that makes a named worker panic at its next park SHALL be public behind a test-support cargo feature that is off by default, so an embedder's red runs the real panic, guard and latch.

**Acceptance:**
- The red's three observations at the base, quoted from its run, then the same test green at the final commit, with the latched worker name and message asserted by value.
- A test that panics two workers at once shows one latched failure, naming the first, and the callback run exactly once.
- A drift injection: with the guard's latch call removed, the green test fails, quoted in the handback.
- A callback registered before the panic runs once on the dying worker's thread, and one registered after the latch runs at once on the registering thread, each observed by its own event (lead amendment B).
- An integration test outside the crate, built with the test-support feature, panics a named worker through the public seam and reads the latched failure by value (lead amendment B).

### R3: After a failure, every wait ends with it and shutdown completes

Behavioural. After the failure latches, THE SYSTEM SHALL end every wait an embedder holds on the scheduler with the failure, never by a timeout. Scheduler::run_until_exit SHALL return the failure as an error, a breaking change released in beamr's next minor version. Exit and completion watches SHALL answer as B-179 amendment C has them answer at shutdown (AlreadyRemoved), so haematite's held writes end as it already handles that answer. Scheduler::shutdown SHALL return after a worker died: it joins every thread, takes every process left in the dead worker's local and inject queues, and finalizes it as shutdown finalizes the rest. No process SHALL stay unfinalized because the worker that held it is gone. Lead amendment B (Artemis, 4 November 2026, with Waffles' condition): after the latch, shutdown and drop SHALL NOT wait on a worker that died or did not answer the wake. Whether a worker is waited on SHALL be decided by events, never by a join with a timeout: a worker whose death the latch has recorded, or whose thread has reported its own exit, is skipped, its handle detached, and its name given in shutdown's log line; every other worker is joined as before. The dead worker's queued processes are finalized as R3 has it either way.

**Acceptance:**
- With one worker panicked, a run_until_exit held on another thread returns the failure, and shutdown returns, each observed by its own completion event, never by a sleep.
- A process queued on the dead worker's local queue and one on its inject queue are both finalized by shutdown, asserted by their watches' answers.
- The handback lists every public method that waits, from a search of the crate quoted in it, with the test that shows each one ending on the failure.
- A worker held wedged by the test-support seam and a worker the latch has recorded dead do not hold up shutdown or drop: each returns, observed by its own completion event, and shutdown's log line names the skipped workers (lead amendment B).
- A drift injection: with the latch's record no longer consulted, the held-wedged test does not complete within the nextest profile's terminate, quoted in the handback (lead amendment B).

### R4: The park no longer panics, by the remedy R1's red names, and the release says what changed

If R1 shows a kernel psynch error on valid arguments, the amendment chooses a park that reports an error rather than asserting. Two candidates are named here and neither is chosen: std::thread::park_timeout with unpark (on macOS a different primitive from the pthread condvar), or a wait-on-address park, under the no-unsafe rule. If R1 reproduces nothing, the amendment says so, and R2 and R3 ship without R4's code. ### R5: A dead worker is recovered where its state can be accounted for, and every death is reported

Behavioural, red first. Lead amendment A (Apollo's draft, landed by Artemis as beamr lead on 4 November 2026, decided by Waffles on Tom's word at 01:1x, "can you run things through, I'm too busy" and "make it work", answering his own question at 00:21: "the whole point of beamr is that when something crashes it's able to deal with that, so why can't we deal with that?"). It replaces the first boundary and adds this requirement. R1, R3 and R4 stand; R2 stands, its latch kept for the third tier. Recovery follows BEAM's supervision: a process that crashes is restarted by its supervisor, and the VM stops only when its own state cannot be trusted. A scheduler worker is part of the VM, so beamr restarts it only where it can show the worker left nothing half-done. R2's guard SHALL record where the worker was when it died: parked (in its park, holding no process), running a named pid, or anywhere else. After the death the recovery SHALL check every scheduler-shared mutex for poison before deciding the tier, and print what it checked and what it found. Tier 1: a worker that died parked SHALL be restarted. The park takes the wait_set guard, changes nothing in the WaitSet, and waits, so a panic there leaves wait_set poisoned with no change of its own half-made. Poison is no proof (Waffles' condition): before the restarted worker takes any queue, the recovery SHALL check the WaitSet's invariants (every waiting pid names a live process in a waiting or suspended state; every woken entry names a live pid), clear the poison only if they hold, and print the figure, entries checked and violations found; if the check cannot be made or fails, the death is tier 3. It SHALL undo what the dead worker left behind (its parked census entry, its idle ticket and its note_wait), and the restarted worker SHALL run on the same index with the same local queue, through its stealer, and the same inject queue. Tier 2: a worker that died running a process SHALL kill that process through the ordinary exit path with the reason worker_crashed (lead amendment A.1), so its links and monitors are told and its supervisor restarts it, and the worker SHALL restart as in tier 1. This applies only when no shared mutex other than that process's own slot is poisoned and the WaitSet check passes; otherwise the death is tier 3. Tier 3: anything else SHALL latch R2's permanent failure, and R3 ends every wait. That is a worker that died anywhere but parked or running a process, a poisoned shared mutex the recovery cannot prove consistent, and a restarted worker that dies again before it has run one process slice. No count and no timer decide this; the event does. Nothing is silent. Every death, of every tier, SHALL be reported to the embedder through a callback registered by Scheduler::on_worker_death, taking an Fn(&WorkerDeath) that is Send, Sync and 'static, run on the dying worker's thread for each death, carrying the worker's name, where it was, the panic message, the tier, the pid crashed (tier 2) and the poison check's figure; Scheduler::worker_deaths SHALL return the count. Scheduler::on_failure (R2, lead amendment B) still runs once, only when a tier-3 death latches the failure. A recovery that looks like normal running is the defect this brief exists to remove, so a restart is reported exactly as loudly as a stop. Lead amendment A.1 (Artemis, beamr lead, 4 November 2026, from her review of R5's code): ExitReason is a Copy enum of atoms and cannot carry text, so tier 2's named reason SHALL be a new unit variant, ExitReason::WorkerCrashed, rendered as the atom worker_crashed wherever exit reasons become atoms; it is a breaking change, carried in this release's CHANGELOG. The worker's name and the panic message SHALL travel in the WorkerDeath that on_worker_death receives, with crashed_pid naming the process, and in the line the death prints. Tier 1's poison clear SHALL be made inside the same hold of the wait_set lock in which the WaitSet's invariants were last proven, and only over the state they were proven on: if the waiting or woken entries changed since the check read them, the check is made again. So no poison is ever cleared that the check did not cover, including a poison left by a second worker's death meanwhile.

**Acceptance:**
- Red first: the R2 seam panics one worker in its park. With the code, the worker restarts on the same index, a process spawned afterwards runs to exit, processes already on the dead worker's local and inject queues run, and on_worker_death reports tier 1 with the WaitSet figure printed.
- Red first: the slice seam panics a worker while it runs a named pid. With the code, that pid exits with the reason worker_crashed, a monitor on it receives the down message carrying worker_crashed, the worker restarts, the scheduler goes on running the other processes, and on_worker_death reports tier 2 with crashed_pid naming that pid, the worker's name and the panic message (lead amendment A.1).
- Red first: with a second shared mutex poisoned by a seam before the park panic, the death latches R2's failure (tier 3), on_failure runs once, and shutdown completes as R3 has it.
- Red first: a restarted worker made to die again before running a slice latches tier 3.
- Every wait in these tests ends on an event (a watch, a down message, a callback), never a sleep or timer.
- Drift injections, each quoted in the handback: the WaitSet check removed, so a seeded violation is restarted through and the test fails; the poison check removed, so the tier-3 case restarts and the test fails.

## Boundaries

- SHALL NOT restart a dead worker except as R5 has it (lead amendment A): only a worker that died parked, after the wait set is proved consistent with a printed figure, or one that died running a process, after that process is killed as a crash. Every other death, any unproven poison, and a second death before a slice latch R2's failure.
- SHALL NOT abort the host process from beamr. The failure goes to the embedder, who decides how its process ends.
- SHALL NOT end any wait by a timer or a fixed sleep, in code or in tests.
- SHALL NOT add unsafe code, or a dependency without its licence and the reason in Cargo.toml.
- SHALL NOT mark any test serial, ignored or skipped to make it pass.
